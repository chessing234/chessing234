His letter describes the work I did on school visits, teacher recruitment, fundraising, and health outreach.
- **[Shortest Path to IOI (SPOI)](https://spoi.org.in/team).** I led editorial work and wrote about 40 competitive programming editorials for students preparing for informatics olympiads. The program offers [free mentorship](https://codeforces.com/blog/entry/142513), and I’m listed on the [SPOI team](https://spoi.org.in/team/taksh-kothari).
- **T.U.R.A.** I helped write a [free competitive programming book](https://github.com/T-U-R-A/tura-coding-book) with explanations and worked solutions for the [CSES Problem Set](https://cses.fi/problemset/).
- **Urban Earth.** With [Ateeque Malani](https://www.che.iitb.ac.in/faculty-page/ateeque-malani) and researchers at IIT Bombay, I built a Markov-chain model of composting. We diverted about 5,000 kg of wet waste and shared compost and citrus bioenzymes with neighbors.

## Research

- **Mantis at MIT CSAIL.** On the [Mantis team](https://kellislab.github.io/MantisDiscovery/lab/) at the Kellis Lab, I led work on knowledge graphs and embeddings and mentored other researchers. I helped build [search and map features](https://mantis.csail.mit.edu/docs/start/read-the-map.html) that let readers explore how ideas connect across large collections of documents and trace findings back to the original passages.
- **Language-model memory.** [Jinha Kim and I](https://dblp.org/pid/78/2099.html#:~:text=Delayed%20Supervision%20for%20Test%2DTime%20Language%20Models) coauthored [Delayed Supervision for Test-Time Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32312). We studied how models retain facts and revise them when they receive new information.
- **[VayuSaathi](https://sciencesociety.in/insef/Online_INSEF_Selection_2025.htm).** I built a low-cost air-quality monitor and filed a provisional patent application. The monitor won [INSEF Silver](https://insef.org/insef/INSEF_Regional_Fair_Online_2025_26_Results.htm) and [CREST Gold](https://www.crestawards.org/secondary-further-education/gold/).
- **[DALTON](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/81e16e41f99fe4ab9c23a0e0da8eaab2-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html).** I worked with IIT Kharagpur’s UbiNet group on [indoor air-quality monitoring](https://ubinet-iitkgp.github.io/ubinet/pages/DALTON). The VayuSaathi–DALTON collaboration received a commendation from the Government of India. UbiNet’s follow-up project, [led by Sandip Chakraborty](https://research.google/programs-and-events/society-centered-ai/google-society-centered-ai-research-awardees/), received a [US$100,000 Google Research award](https://cse.iitkgp.ac.in/Newsletter_010126.pdf#page=2).
- **Formal mathematics.** In Terence Tao’s Lean Analysis project, I worked on [finite additivity of counting measure](https://github.com/teorth/analysis/pull/712) and [scaling finitely additive measures](https://github.com/teorth/analysis/pull/700). I also worked on exponent-pair geometry in [ExpDB](https://teorth.github.io/expdb/), [proof checks in IEANTN](https://github.com/teorth/IEANTN/issues/232), and features for the [Palomar Registry](https://palomar-registry.org/).
- **The Josephus problem.** In [The Continuous Josephus Problem](https://jidmis.org/index.php/jidmis/article/view/4102), I used circle rotations, discrepancy, and Fourier analysis to study how repeated elimination behaves.
- **Physics-informed neural networks.** I compared five ways to choose sampling points across three equations used to model electronic devices. The paper was accepted at [IEEE ICCWN 2026](https://iccwn.in/).
- **SRS Bulgaria.** At the [Summer Research School in Bulgaria](https://srs.math.bas.bg/), I worked on Zarankiewicz problems on hypergrids with [Demira Nedeva](https://bg.linkedin.com/in/demira-nedeva-a0022831a). We looked at how many points a multidimensional grid can contain while avoiding particular patterns.
- **Random graphs with Alan Frieze.** With guidance from [Alan Frieze](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=EqKRQ4YAAAAJ) at Carnegie Mellon, I studied how to quickly find a [Hamiltonian cycle in a random regular graph](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.07139). Such a cycle visits every vertex exactly once before returning to its starting point. Frieze suggested starting with a random graph of degree three and adding random perfect matchings.
